This manuscript targets a recurring applied challenge: reconstructing predator
front dynamics and prey extirpation timing when direct predator abundance data
are sparse, noisy, or unavailable. We use a response-only strategy based on
prey extirpation timing (`TX`) derived from presence-only records and calibrate
process parameters against pattern-oriented summaries.

The key scientific risk is confounding ecological signal with observation
artifacts. To address this, we require a locked synthetic benchmark before any
real-data claim. The benchmark quantifies what is recoverable under known truth
and explicitly maps weak-identifiability and failure regimes.

The current focal system is invasive American mink (*Neovison vison*) and water
vole (*Arvicola amphibius*) on a British National Grid representation. We treat
this as both an ecological case and a methodological stress-test for
response-only inference.
