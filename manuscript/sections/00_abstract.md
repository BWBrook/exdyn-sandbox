## Abstract

We developed and validated a process-explicit, pattern-oriented framework for
reconstructing prey extirpation timing from response-only data under sparse and
biased observation. A synthetic mink→vole twin was used to separate ideal
algorithmic identifiability (`TX_true`) from realistic inference after an
observation model and first-passage TX estimation (`TX_obs`). We then applied
the constrained model to the real mink–water-vole case.

The synthetic benchmark demonstrates where recovery is robust and where it
breaks under low-information regimes. Stress sweeps expose failure regimes
driven primarily by arrival-surface incompleteness and summary compatibility
collapse. In the real-data case study, information audits and representation
diagnostics quantify whether inference is supported at the chosen analysis unit.

All figures and tables are pinned to canonical artefact bundles, and the full
workflow is reproducible via `targets`, `renv`, and Quarto.
